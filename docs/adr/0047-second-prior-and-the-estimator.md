# ADR-0047: A second anatomical prior and the estimator's regime — the sparse random network as the same generator at its widest window; the lag-$k$ slope over a caller's series and a spike train binned at any width in `cortex-homeostasis`; the coarse estimate cross-checked against the train and the fine-bin slopes measured beside the causal ratio on both priors at 256 and 1 024 units; no second line of the record, since the coarse estimate's noise is the bin's and a fine-bin slope reads a driven population's autocorrelation, which follows the gross causal ratio at 1 024 units and not at 256

## Context and Problem Statement

[ADR-0044](0044-reference-network.md) measured the estimator of [ADR-0036](0036-criticality-control.md) on one prior and left an open question in whitepaper §11.1: the lag-one estimate of one window swings by more than itself between two windows at one gain (0.659 then 0.000 at 256 units), the controller turns at the saturation ceiling rather than at a slope of 1, and "a smoothed estimate across windows (an exponential average of the slope, or the multistep regression's sums) needs a line the record does not have since ADR-0037 took its reserved bytes; the round that adds one decides between them on a second prior whose slope reads $\sigma$ below the ceiling, if one exists". The proposal of brief 023 asked for a second topological prior, for whether it "permits population activity where the lag-one slope smoothly tracks $\sigma$ below the saturation ceiling", and, if not, to "formalize whether multi-window exponential smoothing or multi-step regression is mathematically required" and whether smoothing "can be computed caller-side or if an architectural amendment to `HomeostaticDrivePool` is justified once measured".

Three facts shaped the answer. The record's bin is $2^{12}$ ticks, chosen above the wheel's horizon so that every direct descendant of a bin's spikes lands in that bin or the next; on the reference network a generation is a few hundred ticks (local delays of 100 to 300, a unit's latency of about 12 to 128), so a bin is ten to forty generations and the slope of bin against previous bin is the autocorrelation of the population count at that lag, which for a branching process at ratio $m$ is of the order of $m^{\Delta/\tau}$: near zero for anything subcritical, and dominated by the variance of a window's thirty-one pairs (Wilting and Priesemann 2018 assume a bin near the generation time). A caller that holds a spike train, which every fork of ADR-0044 returns and which the executor's trace records, can bin it at any width and regress it at any lag without a record; the record's own estimate is a special case of that regression, which makes the record checkable against the train. And the prior of ADR-0044 is a generator with a window: at the widest window every unit but the source and its antipode is a target with equal probability, nothing is rewired, and every delay is from the local band, which is the sparse random excitatory-inhibitory network of Brunel (2000) with short delays, a second topology with no code for the topology.

## Decision Drivers

- ADR-0016's one-owner rule: the estimator's rule over a caller's series belongs beside the record's estimator in `cortex-homeostasis`; the trains are the runtime's.
- ADR-0036's "What is not claimed" and ADR-0044's open question: a change to the record is the ADR after the measurement that shows what it would read.
- ADR-0010 and principle 3: every number below is the engine's own on a stated prior at a stated size, pinned from one run; what the same rules read at Appendix A's scale is a Target.
- CI's budget: the gate holds one gain, two windows and four kicks on the second prior at 256 units and adds the fine slopes to the lattice's gate; the three-gain sweeps at 256 and 1 024 units are the weekly job's `exhaustive` tests.
- "Latest ≠ Newest": Brunel's network (2000) and the least-squares slope with an intercept are decades old with documented failure modes; the regression at several lags is the multistep estimator's raw material (Wilting and Priesemann 2018), used here to measure, not to control.

## Considered Options

1. A laminar or columnar prior with a new section (kind 38) in the image.
2. **The random network as the same generator at its widest window; `count_bins` and `slope_at_lag` in `cortex-homeostasis`; the exit tests measuring, on both priors, the record's estimate per window, the same estimate recomputed from the train's bins, the slopes at bins of $2^8$ ticks at lags 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16, and the causal ratios; the record unchanged.**
3. A second record line (the sums for lags 1 to $K$ at a finer bin, or an exponential average of the coarse estimate), format 14, and the controller reading it.
4. A finer bin for the record's own estimator (`ACTIVITY_BIN_SHIFT` below the horizon).

## Decision Outcome

Option 2. Option 3 is not taken, for the reason the measurement gives; option 4 is not taken, for the same reason and for the bin's rationale in ADR-0036.

- **The second prior** (`runtime/cortex-runtime/tests/reference.rs`, `random_prior`): `Prior { window: units / 2 - 1, rewire_q0_8: 0, .. }` with the lattice's other parameters (every fifth unit inhibitory at the rail, 32 synapses per unit, delays of 100 to 300 ticks, excitatory weights in [6 000, 12 000], seed 22). Its census at 256 units: 51 inhibitory units, 8 192 synapses, 1 632 inhibitory, no long-range synapse (the window is the ring) and no apical one; every synapse's target is neither its source nor its antipode and every delay is in the local band.
- **The rules** (`cortex-homeostasis`, module `regression`). `count_bins(train, from, width, out)` counts a `(tick, unit)` train's spikes per bin of `width` ticks from `from`, saturating at `ACTIVITY_COUNT_MAX`, a width of zero counting nothing. `slope_at_lag(series, lag)` is the lag-$k$ least-squares slope with an intercept, Q16.16 in $[0, 16]$: a series without a spike is 0; an empty series, a lag of zero, fewer than two pairs, or first members that never varied is no estimate; a negative slope is 0; the slope saturates at `SIGMA_MAX_Q16`; each count is read at most the cap, so the sums fit `i128`. At lag one over a window's bins it is exactly the record's `estimate_branching_ratio`, its cases in its order, which a property walk of two thousand random windows holds; a geometric series has the slope $m^k$ at lag $k$; the roundings are pinned from an oracle outside the tree.
- **The cross-check.** In every measurement the record's estimate per window (the executor's own, read after `regulate`) is compared with `slope_at_lag` over the same window's bins of $2^{12}$ ticks counted from the run's train: they are equal on every window below the ceiling at both sizes on both priors (at the ceiling the record reports `SIGMA_MAX_Q16` and the slope its raw value).
- **What the two priors read** (two workers, the drive of ADR-0044, the modulation baseline at 0, two windows per gain; eight kicks at 256 units and sixteen at 1 024; the estimate and the slopes are Q16.16 in the tests and decimals here; the gate holds the lattice at 2.0 with one window and four kicks and the random network at 2.0, the rest is the weekly form):

  The gate's four-kick rows at 256 units read the lattice at 2.0 with one window (0.659; gross 0.455, net 0.273; fine 0.569, 0.468, 0.338, 0.197, 0.237) and the random network at 2.0 (gross 0.083, net 0; the fine slopes as above). The closed loop from a gain of 1.0 under a step of an eighth on the random network climbs as on the lattice, reaches the ceiling at the ninth window at 256 units (10 878 spikes over 8 192) and at the tenth and the twelfth at 1 024 (53 441 and 50 081 over 32 768), and turns there. Probed and not pinned: on the random network at 256 units the fine lag-one slope reads 0.011 at a gain of 1.5 (29 and 33 spikes per window) and 0.538 at 2.5, where both windows are at the ceiling.
- **What is decided.** *The one-window noise is the bin's, not the window's.* At a bin of ten to forty generations the expected slope is near zero for every gain below the ceiling on both priors at both sizes (0.659 then 0.000; 0.232 then 0.040; 0.633 then 0.000; 0.152 then 0.000 at 26 290 spikes), so the per-window values are the variance of thirty-one pairs around a mean that carries no reading of $\sigma$; an exponential average across windows converges to that mean (the averages of the pinned pairs run from 0.06 to 0.37), not to $\sigma$, and a caller can compute it and gain nothing. *A fine bin reads the population's autocorrelation, which is not $\sigma$ at 256 units and moves with the gross causal ratio at 1 024.* The slopes at bins of 256 ticks decay with the lag as a branching process would (on the random network 0.68, 0.52, 0.19, 0, 0 at 256 units; on the lattice more slowly, with a rise at lag sixteen where the far band's descendants land). At 256 units the lag-one value does not follow the causal ratio across gains: on the random network it reads 0.68 where the oracle attributes two descendants to twenty-one ancestor spikes (gross 0.10) and 0.59 where the gross ratio is 0.46. At 1 024 units it rises with the gain as the gross ratio does and stays within 0.15 of it on the random network (0.12 against 0.21, 0.55 against 0.58, 0.86 against 0.71) and within 0.3 on the lattice (0.35 against 0.53, 0.64 against 0.64, 0.70 against 1.00), and reads above the net ratio everywhere (0.12 to 0.48). The regression reads a driven population's autocorrelation, which has two sources: the propagation of spikes through synapses, which $\sigma$ counts, and the re-firing of a unit the drive holds near its threshold, whose basal potential outlives its refractory window; at 256 units the second dominates the first, at 1 024 less so, and no regression over the count separates them, which the oracle does by construction. *The record stays as it is.* A second line, whether the coarse estimate's average or the sums for several lags at a finer bin, would carry a number that reads $\sigma$ at neither size the tree can run in its gate and is of the gross ratio's order at the size the weekly job runs; a controller acting on it would act on the re-firing as much as on the propagation. At this scale the ceiling stays the actuator's bound, as ADR-0044 recorded, and the loop's behaviour is unchanged. What reads $\sigma$ in the loop is a causal count; the way to put one inside the engine without forks is an attribution the engine keeps per delivery (an ancestor stamp a message carries into the mailbox, tallied at the spike), Specified here and not built. The size dependence is the measurement the next size gives: the round that adds a line runs the same sweep at 4 096 units first and takes the line only if the fine slope reads the gross ratio there within the gate's tolerance. The open question of §11.1 is resolved: not by a line of the record, by the measurement that says which line would help and at which size.
- **Not adopted.** *Option 1*: no measurement asks for a laminar prior, and section kind 38 stays Specified. *Option 3*: the reason above. *Option 4*: a bin below the horizon reads a delayed descendant several bins later, which is the multistep regression's case, and the fine slopes show what it would read. *A drive-free measurement* (the network's own activity without the stationary drive, where re-firing would not confound the slope): the reference network is silent without its drive at every gain below the ceiling (the loop's first windows at a gain of 1.0 count 0, 0, 0 spikes), so the confound is the regime's.

### Consequences

- Good: the open question of ADR-0044 has a measured answer on two topologies at two sizes, and the answer is that the record needs nothing at this scale; a decision that would have cost a format bump is not taken, with the numbers that say why and the measurement that would reverse it.
- Good: the record's estimator is now checkable against the spike train by an independent rule, and is checked on every window of the exit tests.
- Good: a second prior costs no crate code; the generator's window is the topology's parameter.
- Bad: the fine-bin slopes are pinned from one run; the sweeps are the weekly job's, and the size at which the fine slope reads the gross ratio is not yet run.
- Bad: the confound of re-firing is inferred from the regression's disagreement with the oracle at 256 units, not measured on its own; a population-wide count of a unit's second spike within a basal time constant against a spike a message caused is the Specified attribution.

## Alternatives considered and why rejected

- **The estimator's window lengthened** (more pairs per window): the mean it converges to is still the autocorrelation at ten to forty generations.
- **The multistep fit** ($r_k = b\,m^k$ fitted over $k$): its raw material is pinned here; the fit would read the same autocorrelation.
- **The second prior in `cortex-connectome` as a named constructor**: a struct literal with two fields changed is the prior; a constructor would name a topology the tests already name.

## Confirmation

`cortex-homeostasis` (`regression.rs`): a halving series at every lag to the last pair, a doubling one, a constant drive added; the edges (no spike at one, two and four bins, silence at any lag, no bin, one bin, one pair, first members that never varied, a negative slope, the ceiling, the counts' cap, the pairs without a spike between them); the roundings up and down; a window's series against the record's estimate and silent and constant windows; the bins from a start at a width, saturating, a width of zero, the clock's width; the property walk over two thousand random windows. `tests/reference.rs`: the random prior's census and its synapses' bounds; the gate's rows above with the cross-check; the `exhaustive` sweeps on both priors at 256 and 1 024 units with the closed loop on the random network. The mutation gate on the changed lines (ADR-0030) passes in CI.
