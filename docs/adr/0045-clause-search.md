# ADR-0045: The executive clause search — the candidate pairs intra-construction can take and a bounded proof search in `cortex-reasoning`, the runtime's search over a clause store that commits an invention whose reward is positive and undoes every other; the symbolic half of H-11 decided, the synaptic half open; the lexicon and the cortico-striatal gating not adopted

## Context and Problem Statement

[ADR-0041](0041-induction-on-the-term-arena.md) gave `cortex-reasoning` the inverse-resolution operators and [ADR-0043](0043-discovery-path.md) gave the runtime `invent`, which intra-constructs two clauses, measures the store's description length before and after, and turns the drop into a valence and a reward. Every operator waits for a caller to choose its two inputs; nothing in the tree walks a store, and hypothesis H-11 asks whether a rewarded invention is ever read back by a later behaviour. The proposal of brief 022 asked for a `ClauseSearchQueue` in `cortex-executive` over `TermNode` slices with pairing heuristics (a shared head functor, overlapping literal hashes, co-activation), wired to `invent`, and an exit test holding that a synapse consolidated by the reward "alters subsequent firing trajectories or accelerates future resolution/deduction queries involving the newly invented predicate".

Three facts shaped the answer. A state crate declares no dependency (TC-2, `scripts/check-deps.mjs`), so a rule over terms cannot live in `cortex-executive`: the pair walk and a proof search are rules over the term arena and belong to `cortex-reasoning`; the composition with the affect state, the store and the reward belongs to the runtime. "Read back" has two halves that nothing in the tree joins: a proof over the store reads an invented predicate exactly when its path passes through the invented clauses, which a proof search can count; a synapse the reward consolidated biases a later behaviour only if some rule maps the invention's id to a pattern of units, and no such rule exists (the ledger's `Episode` of [ADR-0038](0038-episodic-ledger-and-replay.md) is the record such a rule would write). And `update_valence` primes the affect state to the length it was given, so a caller that rejects an invention must put the state back itself.

## Decision Drivers

- ADR-0016's rule that a quantity has one owner, and TC-2: the pair walk and the proof search in `cortex-reasoning`, the search that commits and rewards in the runtime.
- ADR-0043's contract for `invent`: `NotPrimed` unless the affect state's previous free energy is the store's length, `NotInStore` unless both inputs are in the store; a caller that composes several inventions keeps that contract at every step.
- The identity of ADR-0041 (an operator's outputs resolve back to its inputs) is what makes a committed invention safe for later proofs: every proof through the two inputs maps to a proof through the three outputs, one step longer per invented predicate on the path.
- ADR-0010 and principle 3: what a search over a store of twenty-four clauses commits is stated; what it does over a store a later round builds is not.
- The whitepaper's §5.2.30 Specified list names "clause search" and "standardising apart"; this decision takes the first and leaves the second, saying why.

## Considered Options

1. The proposal: a queue in `cortex-executive` with hashed-literal and co-activation heuristics, and an exit test on firing trajectories.
2. **The candidate pairs as a walk over the store in index order (both clauses of two literals or more, their heads of one shape through the bindings) and a bounded depth-first proof search with the leftmost literal selected and backtracking over the clause, both in `cortex-reasoning`; in the runtime a search that walks the pairs, commits an invention whose reward is positive (the inputs replaced by the common clause and the first definition, the second definition appended, the walk restarted) and undoes every other with the affect state put back; the exit test holds the proofs before and after the search and the modulator's coupling; the synaptic half of H-11 stays open.**
3. A best-first search over pairs ordered by the saving they would give, measured before committing.
4. A search that commits every invention the operator produces and lets the reward alone reach the modulator.

## Decision Outcome

Option 2.

- **`next_pair(store, after, scratch)`** (`cortex-reasoning`, `induce.rs`): the next `(i, j)` with `i < j` in index order, after `after` when there is one, whose clauses both have two literals or more (intra-construction needs a common literal and a differing one in each) and whose heads are of one shape through the bindings: two constants of one id, two compounds of one functor and arity, or either a variable, so that the heads can unify. `Malformed` for an entry that is not a clause; `None` when no pair is left. The walk stops at the first pair, before any entry it does not reach.
- **`resolve_literal(goal, rule, index, scratch)`**: the definite-clause resolution step on a chosen literal, `NoMatch` unless the rule's head unifies with that literal; `resolve_definite` is now the same step on the first literal that unifies. **`prove(goal, store, frames, budget, scratch)`**: a depth-first proof search from `goal` (a clause whose body is what is to be proved; its head is not read) over `store` in index order; each step is `resolve_literal` on the goal's leftmost literal with the next clause whose head unifies with it (SLD resolution's selection: the clause is the only choice point, so a goal of $k$ literals with a fact for each is not searched in $k!$ orders, which the first draft did and spent a budget of 4 096 on a store of twenty-four clauses), backtracking over the choice of clause by restoring the mark taken before it. `Some(Proof { steps, resolutions })` at the empty body, `None` when every choice is spent; `frames` (`Frame { goal, next, mark }`) is the depth bound, `BoundExceeded` past it, and `budget` bounds the resolutions attempted, `InduceError::Budget` past it. The scratch is left as it was, proof or not: the answer is whether, and in how many steps, not the substitution. A clause is not renamed between uses, so a proof that needs one clause twice under two bindings is not found: standardising apart stays Specified, and a caller whose store needs it knows by a proof that is not found. `InduceMark` gains `Default` so that a frame slice can be built.
- **`search(store, len, scratch, affect, budget, out)`** (the runtime, `discovery.rs`): the pairs `next_pair` yields, in order, each through `invent`. A discovery whose reward is positive is committed: the store's slot `i` takes the common clause, slot `j` the first definition, slot `len` the second (`StoreFull` when the slice has no slot, with the invention undone), the discovery is written to `out` (`OutFull` likewise), the report's length and reward total move, and the walk restarts from the first pair, since the store changed. A discovery whose reward is not positive is undone: the scratch restored to before the operator ran and the affect state put back as it was, so the valence rule keeps no trace of a change that did not happen. A pair the operator refuses for a reason of its own (`NoMatch`, `NothingToInvent`, `TooManyArguments`, `BodyFull`, `PairsFull`) is a rejection and the walk goes on; the operator's other errors (a malformed store, an arena or a band exhausted, a walk past its limit) end the search with the store as it was before that attempt. The walk ends when no pair is left or `budget` attempts are spent. `SearchReport { attempts, commits, rejections, length_before, length_after, reward_total_q16 }`; the committed rewards are the caller's to pass to `Executor::reward`, as ADR-0043 left them, and their sum saturates. The affect state must be primed to the store's length before the search, as `invent` requires; after a commit the valence update has primed it to the new length.
- **What the exit test holds** (`runtime/cortex-runtime/tests/discovery.rs`). A store of three clauses of one head sharing four literals of arity one and differing in a fifth, one clause of another head, and the facts that make `p(k)` hold for three constants and fail for a fourth: 106 nodes. Before the search each of the three goals is proved in six steps (the clause, then five facts) and the fourth is not. The search tries two pairs and commits both (the first pair, then the common clause with the third clause): 106 nodes become 100, two rewards of three quarters, the store six clauses longer by two, no pair left. After the search the same three goals are proved and the fourth still is not; their proofs are eight, eight and seven steps: the first two pass through both invented predicates, the third through one. The two rewards into the modulator of the two-unit network sum to 1.5, and the next presynaptic spike consolidates the pending trace under a modulation clamped to 1.0. Unit tests hold the walk's order and its refusals, the proof search's backtracking, its bounds and that the scratch is left as it was after a proof, a failure and every refusal, and the search's commit, its undo with the affect state put back, its two full errors and its bounds.
- **What is decided.** H-11's symbolic half: an invention the search commits is read back by every later proof whose path it shortens the store on, at one step per invented predicate, and no goal is lost or gained. Its synaptic half stays open: the reward consolidates whatever trace is pending, and nothing maps the invention's id to a pattern of units, so no behaviour of the network can be said to read it. The rule that would close it writes an `Episode` from an invention (the pattern active when the reward came, tagged for replay), which is a decision for a round that has a representation of an id in the population; this one does not.
- **Not adopted.** *Option 1*: the crate TC-2 forbids, heuristics no measurement asks for, and an exit test on firing trajectories that any weight change passes. *Option 3*: a best-first order needs every pair measured before one is committed, which is the search's whole cost again per commit; the index order is deterministic and the walk restarts on a commit, which finds what a best-first order finds on the exit test's store and the ADR does not claim more. *Option 4*: an invention that lengthens the store is a negative valence the affect record would carry and a reward the modulator would take for a change the caller does not keep. *The lexicon* (the proposal's third pillar): a lookup from a token id to a category term on the arena is the whole of it, it is not on the path of any hypothesis this round decides, and it is the next brief's first item. *The cortico-striatal gating circuit*: no measurement asks for it, `BasalGangliaChannelState::compute_gating` is one rule with no caller, and the frame's roles (`ROLE_SUBJECT`, `ROLE_ACTION`, `ROLE_OBJECT`, `ROLE_AFFECT`, not the proposal's agent, patient, action and goal) are bound by the reduction of [ADR-0040](0040-categorial-reduction.md) already. *The search's budget in the amendment registry*: a caller's argument this round.

### Consequences

- Good: the operators of ADR-0041 have a caller that chooses their inputs, and the store it leaves is one every earlier proof still goes through.
- Good: `cortex-reasoning` has its first proof search; a proof's length is a number a test can pin, which is what "read back" needed to mean anything.
- Good: no record, no field, no format bump; the pin holds.
- Bad: the walk is index order and restarts on every commit, $O(n^2)$ pairs per commit over a store of $n$ clauses; a store of a thousand clauses is a later round's, with the measurement that says which order it needs.
- Bad: the proof search does not rename, so a store whose proofs need one clause twice reports no proof; standardising apart stays Specified with that as its symptom.
- Bad: H-11's synaptic half is as open as before; this decision says what closes it.

## Alternatives considered and why rejected

- **A pair table computed once**: the store changes on every commit, and the walk over pairs is cheaper than keeping a table consistent.
- **Renaming inside `prove`**: a fresh copy of a clause per use is an arena allocation per step and a variable band per proof; the round that needs it takes it with a store that shows the symptom.
- **A reward for every attempt**: the reward is the valence of a change the store keeps.

## Confirmation

`cortex-reasoning`: `next_pair` on a store of five clauses and a fact (the pairs in order, a one-literal clause and a fact skipped, one clause and an empty store, a position past the end, arities as shapes, a variable head, a malformed entry reached and one not reached); `resolve_literal` on the chosen literal only, past the body, and beside `resolve_definite`; `prove` on a store with two rules and four facts (three steps, no proof after a backtrack, a proof after a failed branch, two literals, an empty goal, an empty store, the depth bound at three and four frames, the budget, no frames, a malformed entry, the scratch left as it was after each). The runtime: the search's two commits and its restart, a second search that does nothing, an invention that does not pay undone with the affect state put back, a budget of zero, an unprimed state, a length past the store, `StoreFull` and `OutFull` undone; `tests/discovery.rs` as above. The mutation gate on the changed lines (ADR-0030) passes in CI.
