# ADR-0049: Dale's principle in plasticity — a block's polarity is its presynaptic unit's flag, taken by the rules of `cortex-core` as an argument; the eligibility trace is the change of the weight's magnitude and consolidation keeps the weight within the polarity's half of the width; an inhibitory block takes the symmetric window of Vogels et al. 2011 with a depression per presynaptic spike from a stated target rate; finding F-36 resolved and the nights pinned again

## Context and Problem Statement

Finding F-36 (brief 023): the pair rule of [ADR-0022](0022-synapse-fan-out-and-stdp.md) added its amounts to a weight whatever its sign, so the replay of [ADR-0038](0038-episodic-ledger-and-replay.md) carried the six inhibitory synapses among the 1 024-unit network's own captured pattern from −77 824 in sum to twenty times the positive rail, and, in the other direction, depressed six of unit 0's eleven excitatory synapses at 256 units to the negative rail. A unit's sign was a flag the runtime set at synthesis (`FLAG_INHIBITORY`, "read by the runtime, not by this method") and no rule read; Dale's principle held by construction of the prior and by nothing after the first consolidation. Whitepaper §11.1 named the two answers: "whether inhibitory synapses take a rule of their own (Vogels et al. 2011: a symmetric window that potentiates toward a target rate) or the pair rule clamps at zero by sign".

Two facts fix where the answer goes. A block is one presynaptic unit's fan-out, so its polarity is the unit's and one argument serves every slot; the executor holds the unit at the spike and passes the flag, and the rule of `cortex-core` reads no unit (ADR-0016). And the consolidation of ADR-0032 already conserves the sum of weight and trace with a rail on each side; the change is which rails and on which axis.

## Decision Drivers

- Principle 5: a structural boundary beats a reviewed one. A sign held by the rule cannot be lost by a replay; one held by the prior is lost by the first pairing that crosses zero.
- ADR-0016's one-owner rule: the sign is the unit's flag; a bit per synapse would be a second owner of the same fact.
- Latest ≠ Newest (§2.1): the symmetric inhibitory rule has fifteen years of use in the network simulators (NEST's `vogels_sprekeler_synapse`, Brian's and Auryn's examples) with its failure mode documented (a target rate set against the population's regime drives inhibition to a rail); the asymmetric pair rule on a signed weight has no literature behind it for inhibition.
- ADR-0010: the nights of `tests/reference.rs` are the engine's numbers; a deliberate change to the dynamics moves them and says why, and the determinism pin moves only if its run is touched.

## Considered Options

1. The pair rule as it was, with the weight clamped at zero by the sign it started with (the sign read from the weight, undefined at zero).
2. **The polarity from the presynaptic unit's flag as an argument of `step_stdp` and `consolidate`; the trace as the change of the weight's magnitude; consolidation within `[0, i16::MAX]` of the magnitude under the polarity's sign; the excitatory block under ADR-0022's asymmetric rule, the inhibitory block under the symmetric rule with a constant depression per presynaptic spike.**
3. A sign bit per synapse in the block (format 14).
4. The asymmetric rule on the magnitude for both polarities (pre-before-post strengthens inhibition, post-before-pre weakens it).

## Decision Outcome

Option 2; no record changes, format 13; the determinism pin untouched, since its network sets no flag and its run crossed zero nowhere.

- **The polarity** (`cortex-core`, `synapse.rs`). `Polarity { Excitatory, Inhibitory }` with `Polarity::of_flags(flags)` from `FLAG_INHIBITORY` (the burst flag is not it). The executor's fan-out phase reads `Polarity::of_flags(pre.flags)` once per spiking unit and passes it to `step_stdp_all` and `consolidate_all`; the oracle blocks of the sleep and modulation tests and the benchmark pass `Excitatory`.
- **The magnitude and the half-range** (`consolidate(slot, modulation, polarity)`). The trace is the change of the weight's magnitude: `round(trace × m)` is added to the magnitude (the weight for an excitatory block, its negation for an inhibitory one), clamped to `[0, i16::MAX]`, and the weight is the magnitude with the polarity's sign; the trace keeps what the rail did not absorb, so the sum of magnitude and trace is conserved at either rail as ADR-0032 conserved the weight's. The inhibitory rail is therefore −32 767, the magnitude's width, where the prior already saturates (`inhibitory_gain_q4_4` at 255). A weight on the wrong side of zero for its polarity reads as a magnitude of zero and is brought to zero by the first consolidation, at any modulation including zero, with the trace untouched: a malformed arena is made well-formed at the first spike rather than propagated.
- **The two rules** (`step_stdp(slot, pre_now, post_last, polarity)`). Excitatory: ADR-0022 as it was, `+A_+ (1 - 2^{-11})^{q-p}` when the target's last spike $q$ follows the previous presynaptic spike $p$, `−A_- (1 - 2^{-11})^{t-q}` when $q$ precedes this spike $t$. Inhibitory (Vogels et al. 2011 at the nearest-neighbour stamps the block keeps): `−α` at every presynaptic spike, then `+A_+ (1 - 2^{-11})^{q-p}` for the first pairing and `+A_+ (1 - 2^{-11})^{t-q}` for the second, both potentiating the magnitude whichever way round. The constants: the amplitude is $A_+$ (328, ≈ 0.0100) in both terms; `ISTDP_TARGET_PERIOD_TICKS` is 20 000, a target rate of 5 Hz at the 10 µs tick (Vogels' cortical target); `ISTDP_ALPHA_Q1_15` is $2 \rho_0 \tau A_+ = 2 \cdot 328 \cdot 2^{11} / 20\,000$ = 67 (≈ 0.0020), tied by a `const` assertion. A target that fires at the rate is potentiated as much as it is depressed on average; one that fires above it sees its inhibition grow, one below sees it shrink toward zero. The target rate is a constant of the crate this round; a round that tunes it makes it a `REGISTRY` entry by its own ADR.
- **What the exit tests hold** (`tests/reference.rs`, the nights of ADR-0044 and ADR-0048 at 256 and 1 024 units, pinned again from the run under this rule; every number not named here stood: the stages, the replays, the depotentiations, the tags, the invention's and the background's patterns, the bursts, the readout units on every image, and the whole 1 024-unit night). At 256 units the cluster's synapses sum to 1 281 024 before the night (1 281 021 under the old rule) and every one is at the rail after it; the capture night counts 437 spikes to the reward's tick (436); the invention's 127 synapses sum to 1 117 808 before (1 117 805) and 3 605 986 after (3 193 610): 107 at the rail, the eleven from unit 0 at zero where six of them were at the negative rail and five near −23 400, since unit 0 fires in the other episode's replays too, the pair rule at those spikes finds its targets' last spikes a ripple earlier and the half-range holds the depression at zero, and the nine onto unit 0 near their prior weights (8 300 to 14 091); the network's own 45 sum to 175 290 before (175 285) and 846 292 after (825 276), the five from its inhibitory unit 254 at the negative rail; the readout after the night fires the same five units with 30 spikes (32). At 1 024 units the invention's 143 synapses end at the rail as before; the network's own twenty sum to −77 818 before (−77 824) and 262 136 after (655 340, every one at the positive rail under the old rule): fourteen at the positive rail and six, the ones from its inhibitory units 494, 499 and 484, at the negative rail. A replayed pattern that holds inhibitory units now consolidates its inhibition as inhibition.
- **What is decided.** *F-36 is resolved by construction*: no rule can move a weight across zero, and the sign is the unit's flag wherever the block is. *The inhibitory rule is the symmetric one*, since the tree's open item named it first, the simulators carry it, and its one parameter is stated with its derivation; what it does to the reference network's inhibitory weights over the two or three waking bins of the exit tests is small (the 1 024-unit own pattern's sum moves by six in 77 824 over that phase) and what it does over a night is the rail, as every replayed synapse reaches. *The excitatory rule is untouched* in its amounts, its window and its stamps; the one change an excitatory synapse sees is the floor at zero.
- **Not adopted.** *Option 1*: a weight of zero has no sign, and a sign read from the weight is the defect in another form. *Option 3*: a bit per synapse is a second owner of the unit's flag and a format bump for a fact the flag already states. *Option 4*: an asymmetric rule for inhibition has one report behind it (Haas et al. 2006, entorhinal cortex) against the symmetric rule's use across the field; the choice can be revisited by a round with a measurement that distinguishes them, which this round's nights do not (a replayed pattern fires together, where both rules potentiate).

### Consequences

- Good: Dale's principle is a property of the rule, held by a unit test at every rail and by the property walk over both polarities, and no replay, reward or trial can undo it.
- Good: the nights' numbers moved only where a synapse used to cross zero, and the readouts, the patterns and the determinism pin stood: the change is what it says it is.
- Good: an inhibitory synapse now has a rule with a literature and one parameter, rather than the excitatory rule's mirror image by accident of representation.
- Bad: the target rate is a constant chosen from the literature, not from the reference network's regime (which fires its units at 8 to 30 Hz under the drive at a gain of 2.0); a rate below the regime grows inhibition on every active target over a long waking run, which the exit tests' three bins do not show. The interaction with the criticality controller (two rules that move the population's activity, one per synapse and one global) is not measured; the round that runs a waking day on the reference network measures it and sets the rate.
- Bad: the numbers of ADR-0044 and ADR-0048 for the nights stand in those documents as the rule had them; the whitepaper and the tests carry the numbers as the tree has them now.

## Alternatives considered and why rejected

- **A polarity read from the weight's sign at zero as excitatory**: the 1 024-unit own pattern's inhibitory synapses depressed to zero would have become excitatory at the next potentiation.
- **A loader check that every block's weights match its unit's polarity**: a chain walk at load for a fact the first consolidation restores; a round that adds the term arena's sections to the image adds the loader's checks together.
- **The inhibitory amplitude as its own constant**: it equals $A_+$, and a constant that equals another is a mutant no test can tell apart.

## Confirmation

`cortex-core` (`synapse.rs`): the half-range at zero and at both rails for both polarities, a weight on the wrong side brought to zero at any modulation, the inhibitory amounts at the five deltas of the excitatory table in both orders and with no target spike on record, the `_all` forms carrying the polarity to every slot, the flag's reading, two blocks under the same history with the polarity drawn per step, and the property walk (a step moves the trace by at most the windows and the constant, the weight stays on its polarity's side, the magnitude and the trace conserve their sum at a rail too, across the tick wrap). `tests/reference.rs`: the four nights pinned again. `tests/differential.rs`: the pin unmoved. The mutation gate on the changed lines (ADR-0030) passes in CI.
