# ADR-0066: The reward path measured again, through the calibrated instrument — the same rule, the same selection, the same four controls and the same seeds as ADR-0060, on the reference network at 256 and 1 024 units under a criterion over the last 128 trials written before the run, whose rates an integer oracle computes; the rewarded run reads 53 and 49 correct against the 80 asked for and the mirrored assignment 53 and 65, the controls hold and the run is the same on one worker and on four, so the rewarded clause fails at both sizes with the calibration passed; the readings say the instrument resolves the stimulus from the background but not one stimulus from the other, that every coupling falls alike under every feedback, and that at 256 units the resolution is lost within a block once the weights move; no constant moves, and what a next round would change is named and not built

## Context and Problem Statement

[ADR-0065](0065-the-instrument-recalibrated.md) built the four changes [ADR-0060](0060-the-reward-path-measured.md) named and calibrated them as one instrument before any reward was given: at 256 units the readout saw the stimulus in 58 trials of 64 at a gain of 2.0 with the weights frozen, at 1 024 units in 62 at 1.75. This decision asks brief 027's question again through it, whitepaper §11.1's H-12: does a reward through `Executor::reward`, consolidating the eligibility traces pending at its moment, change which of two readouts the engine selects for a stimulus? The rule is [ADR-0032](0032-three-factor-plasticity.md)'s, untouched; the selection `cortex-basal-ganglia`'s linear gate through [ADR-0059](0059-a-task-a-readout-and-a-reward.md)'s task; the controls are brief 027's four (a shuffled reward, a fixed modulation, the mirrored assignment, a second worker count); the seeds are brief 027's (the prior at 22, the trials at 27). What is different is the instrument, and what the difference buys is the meaning of the answer: with the calibration passed, a no is a statement about the rule on this task at these sizes, not about a readout that could not see.

The decision rule was written before the run, in the same commit as the instrument's constants (`4f2da6a`), and is applied as written; the first commit that holds a rewarded run's outcome is `1780b38`. What the numbers say beyond the rule is a reading, and no constant of the task, the instrument or any rule was moved after the run it was measured in (brief 024's lesson, [ADR-0051](0051-the-estimator-at-4096-units.md); brief 025's, [ADR-0054](0054-the-causal-count-inside-the-loop.md); brief 027's, ADR-0060).

## Decision Drivers

- [ADR-0010](0010-measured-or-target.md) and principle 3: every number here is the engine's own on a stated network at a stated size, pinned from one run and held on every worker count and architecture; what the same loop does at Appendix A's scale is a Target with the same generator; "learns" is not used of the engine outside the task, the accuracy, the trials and the controls that produced it.
- The criterion's statistics computed, not assumed: at chance a block of 64 has a standard deviation of four trials, so brief 027's clause over two blocks passed by noise about once in 21 and failed a control about once in 3 (exact enumeration; ADR-0060's "one in twenty-five" and "one in four" were the normal approximation). Over the last 128 trials the binomial's tail gives 80 or more about once in 337 per run and 77 or more about once in 74 per control, so the rewarded clause at both assignments and both sizes passes by noise about once in $1.3 \times 10^{10}$ and one of four controls exceeds its ceiling about once in 19; `the_criterion_reads_as_written` computes the tails in `u128` by Pascal's rule, against Python's `math.comb` apart from the tree.
- [ADR-0061](0061-the-learning-runs-leave-the-gate.md) and [ADR-0058](0058-the-weekly-sweep-and-its-timeouts.md): a run in the gate is paid again by every runtime mutant of the weekly sweep. The full runs and the calibrations are weekly `exhaustive` tests; the gate gains one run, the rewarded run's first block at 256 units, held to the first row of the weekly run's pinned table.
- Legenstein, Pecevski and Maass 2008 (the conditions under which reward-modulated STDP ascends the expected reward): a global scalar can separate two synapses only where their traces differ; Izhikevich 2007; Frémaux and Gerstner 2016; Gurney, Prescott and Redgrave 2001 (§2.1).

## Considered Options

1. **The protocol below, written first, run at both sizes as weekly `exhaustive` tests with the rewarded run's first block in the gate, its outcome recorded whatever it is.**
2. The protocol with a constant moved after a rewarded run (a longer window, a third gain, a larger reward, a smaller readout) and the run repeated.
3. A per-unit learning signal, a structural rule or a different task where the global scalar cannot separate the traces.

## Decision Outcome

### Consequences

- Good: the second falsifiable answer to whether a reward changes what this engine does exists in the tree, is no at 256 and 1 024 units under a rule written first whose rates are computed, and means more than the first: the readout was shown to see the stimulus before any reward was given, so the no is about the rule on this task at these sizes.
- Good: the readings locate what the rule would need, in terms a next round can act on and this round did not build: a signal that reaches the readout that fired, a rule that adds a synapse, a task that asks for a sign rather than a difference, a background under which the pairings can rise.
- Good: every run is pinned block for block with its trace and is the same on one worker and on four; the criterion's rates are an integer oracle's, in the gate.
- Neutral: the gate's runtime tests gain one run of $2^{20}$ ticks at 256 units and four tests that run nothing heavy; the weekly job gains ten runs of $2^{23}$ ticks and two of $2^{20}$.
- Bad: the answer is no again, and H-11's synaptic half waits on it; the debt of ADR-0059 (a learning run is not resumable from an image) stands.
- Bad: at 256 units the calibrated instrument does not survive the first block of the pair rule's drift, so a reading there after the first block is a reading through a readout that no longer sees; the 1 024-unit run carries the decision's weight.

## Alternatives considered and why rejected

- **Option 2**: a constant chosen from a rewarded run and the run repeated is the tuning after the run that ADR-0051, ADR-0054 and ADR-0060 name as the lesson; the reading names what such a constant would meet (traces alike into both readouts, net depression), which no window, gain or reward magnitude changes.
- **Option 3**: outside brief 029's empowerment ("no new learning rule, no per-unit learning signal, no surrogate gradient, no e-prop, no reward predictor, no structural plasticity, no sensory driver"); named above as what a next round would change, which is the whole of this round's licence about it.
- **A criterion over the whole run or over the first block**: the last 128 trials are where a change would have accumulated, and the first block is the gate's pin, not a clause.
- **Counting a tie as half correct**: a tie selects nothing, and a behaviour that selects nothing is not the rewarded one; the rate is read and stated instead.
