# ADR-0080: The reinforced form — H-14 written before any run: on the network ADR-0077 settled, with the baseline at zero, the task as ADR-0059 and ADR-0068 built it (the reward addressed to the synapses from the presented stimulus onto the readout the engine selected, its sign the outcome's) is asked to move the selection to the answer, in the assignment and the mirrored assignment, over three of the instrument's runs; the length derived from H-13's blocks before any run, a wrong selection's pair shown by the rules never to consolidate, the shuffled reward read for lock-in rather than bounded by a clause, the relation to H-12 and the stopping rule written first

## Context and Problem Statement

[ADR-0079](0079-the-rewards-direction-measured.md) read H-13 yes: on the network [ADR-0077](0077-the-background-side.md) settled, with the modulation baseline at zero, a reward delivered after every trial to the synapses from the presented stimulus onto its **assigned** readout, whatever the engine selected, raised those couplings by 12 to 15 per cent over 512 trials and the assigned readout's response above the same trial's without the reward in 128 and 127 trials of the last 128, by six spikes per trial. H-13's stopping rule ([ADR-0078](0078-the-rewards-direction.md), whitepaper §11.1), written before that outcome, names what follows a yes: *"the next decision is an ADR on the reinforced form, a delivery the selection decides, on this network."* This is that ADR. It writes H-14 and builds nothing.

The taught delivery told the engine which synapses to strengthen. The reinforced form does not: the reward reaches the synapses from the presented stimulus onto the readout **the engine selected**, and its sign is whether that selection was the answer. That is the task [ADR-0059](0059-a-task-a-readout-and-a-reward.md) composed and [ADR-0068](0068-the-reward-addressed.md) addressed — `Task::trial` with `Delivery::Addressed` and `Feedback::Answer`, already in `runtime/cortex-runtime/src/task.rs` — which every H-12 round ran on the prior with brief 027's baseline of 0.5 and which never ran on the settled network, because ADR-0077's calibration of the per-trial sign (53 of 64 against 56) stopped it there.

Four readings of ADR-0079 shape the question.

1. **How fast the taught form moved the selection.** The selection toward the assigned readout, per block of 64: 32, 32, 27, 34, 43, 49, 46, 60 in the assignment and 28, 33, 41, 44, 45, 49, 60, 55 in the mirrored assignment, against the withheld arm's 24 to 30. It first passed 40 of 64 in the fifth block and the third, at couplings about 1.03 to 1.05 of the image's; before that it sat at the frozen network's level.
2. **Positive feedback.** The trace's potentiation grew as the couplings rose — on A→R0 the terms per synapse per trial from +0.7 to +4.8, the readout firing 17 spikes per set per volley against 10 — and the rise per block grew over the run and was not settling at the eighth. Neither mechanism ADR-0078 named as bounding it stopped it.
3. **The signal.** Under a reward every trial the dopamine signal's fixed point is 1.712 after a reward and **0.712 at a trial's end** (`decay_dopamine`'s step is the floor of $2^{-14}$ of the signal and at least one LSB, so it decays more slowly than $e^{-1}$ per trial).
4. **The delivery's timing, the same in both forms.** The addressed set is written at a trial's end and stands for the next trial only, and an addressed synapse consolidates at its presynaptic spike; so, in ADR-0068's words, "the reward reaches a stimulus's synapses only at the trials that present it twice running, about half of them".

The question the reinforced form adds is whether the engine, earning its reward by its own selection, gets there: its selection starts at the frozen network's level, so at first only about half its trials are correct, and only a correct trial's pair is rewarded. And the positive feedback ADR-0079 read raises a second question, which has to be answered before the run rather than after it: whether a reward the selection earns can lock the engine onto whatever it happened to choose early.

## Decision Drivers

- H-13's stopping rule, applied as written: the next decision is the reinforced form, on this network.
- **Nothing chosen after a rewarded run**: H-14's configuration, arms, length, criterion, prediction and stopping rule are written here, before its first run, and the round that runs it moves none of them.
- One variable against H-13 where the question allows it — the delivery — and every other constant moved stated with its reason. The run's length is the one: argued below from ADR-0079's blocks, before any run.
- **Lock-in**, read from the rules rather than feared: what a wrong selection's pair receives under the delivery and the signal as the tree has them, derived here, and what a reward without information can do under positive feedback, read by an arm.
- Latest ≠ Newest (§2.1): nothing adopted; the task, the delivery, the feedback, the image and the oracle are in the tree.
- The engine read before a description of it is trusted: `Task::trial`, `Executor::address`, `NeuromodulatorState::modulation`, `NeuromodulatorState::reward` and `decay_dopamine` were read for the derivation below.

## Considered Options

1. **The task as built, on the settled image with the baseline at zero, over 1 536 trials**: `Delivery::Addressed` and `Feedback::Answer` in the assignment and the mirrored assignment, and `Feedback::Shuffled` beside them as a reading.
2. **The same over 512 trials**, the instrument's run, so that the delivery is the only variable moved from H-13.
3. **A reward of one sign**: no reward call after a wrong selection, rather than the answer's negative one.
4. **Brief 027's baseline of 0.5**, the modulation H-12's rewarded runs used.
5. **The shuffled reward as a clause**, bounded at 76 of the last 128 (`CONTROL_MAX`) as in H-12's criterion.
6. **A withheld arm** beside the rewarded ones, as in H-13.
7. **A consolidation at the trial's end** (ADR-0069's third named change), so that a reward reaches its own trial's pairings.

## Decision Outcome

**Option 1.**

- **Why the task as built.** The reinforced form is the delivery ADR-0068 built and every H-12 round ran: after each trial the addressed set is the presented stimulus's units onto the selected readout's units (none at a tie), and the reward is $+1.0$ for a correct selection and $-1.0$ otherwise. No code of the engine or the task is needed; the round composes `Task` on ADR-0077's image.
- **What a wrong selection's pair receives, derived from the rules.** Under the baseline at zero an addressed synapse consolidates under `clamp(0 + signal, 0, 1)` and every other under zero (`Modulations::for_synapse`); `NeuromodulatorState::reward` adds to the signal; `decay_dopamine` moves it toward zero by at most its own magnitude, so it never crosses zero. The signal at a trial's end is at most 0.712, the fixed point of a reward every trial (ADR-0079), and a punishment of 1.0 therefore leaves it negative: after a wrong selection the addressed pair — the wrong one — spends the next trial under a signal below zero and consolidates nothing, and at a tie nothing is addressed. **So in a rewarded arm the synapses from a stimulus onto the readout that is not its answer never consolidate: they, and every synapse outside the two answer pairs, end the run as the image holds them, bit for bit.** The positive feedback ADR-0079 read can therefore run only toward the answer in a rewarded arm; a lock-in onto a wrong readout has no path through the reward. This is arithmetic from the functions and a prediction; the round holds it by assertion, and if it fails, that is a finding about this derivation, not an outcome of H-14.
- **Why not a reward of one sign** (option 3). Without the negative call after a wrong selection, the signal the last correct trial left — up to 0.712 at the trial's end — would still be positive when the task addresses the wrong selection's pair, and would consolidate it through the next trial: a leftover reward for a wrong answer, which is exactly the path to lock-in. The answer's negative reward is what wipes it, and it is already the task's.
- **Why 1 536 trials and not 512** (option 2), argued before any run. The delivery reaches a pair's volley on the same trials in both forms (a stimulus presented twice running); the reinforced form adds that the pair is addressed only after a correct selection. So per trial it delivers about the accuracy times what the taught form delivered, and the accuracy starts at the frozen network's level — H-13's withheld arm selected the assignment's readout in 24 to 30 trials of 64 per block — so at first about 0.45 to 0.5 of the taught rate, rising as the couplings rise. The taught form's selection passed 40 of 64 in the third block in the mirrored assignment and the fifth in the assignment (by trial 192 and trial 320), at couplings about 1.03 to 1.05 of the image's; at the reinforced form's starting rate — about 0.5 of the taught one in the mirrored assignment and 0.45 in the assignment, whose readout the frozen network selects a little less — that is near trial 350 and trial 700. The rest of the rise to where the taught form ended (1.12 to 1.15, the selection 55 to 60 of 64) comes faster as the accuracy climbs, at about 0.7 of the taught rate: near trial 850 and trial 1 000. At a further 30 per cent slower throughout — the signal at a volley after a punishment is below the taught form's fixed point — the crossings land near trial 450 and 900 and the end states near 1 100 and 1 300. At 512 trials the couplings would stand about where the taught form had them after its third or fourth block, where its selection read 27 to 44 of 64: a no that would say the reinforced form is slower than the taught one, which the arithmetic already says, and nothing about whether it gets there. A run of **1 536 trials — three of the instrument's runs, twenty-four blocks** — puts the criterion's last 128 trials (1 409 to 1 536) past every one of those estimates, the slowest by about a hundred trials. The criterion's window, mark and every other constant are H-13's.
- **Why the baseline stays at zero** (option 4). It is the one configuration H-13 read, and under it the only weights that move are the ones the delivery reaches, which is what makes the derivation above an exact prediction and a failure of it a finding. Brief 027's 0.5 would consolidate every synapse at half its trace every trial and resume the settling ADR-0077 stopped; whether learning survives plasticity everywhere is a question for after a yes, named below.
- **Why the shuffled reward is a reading and not a clause** (option 5). Under `Feedback::Shuffled` the reward's sign is a coin the stimulus does not read, and a positive coin rewards whatever the engine selected; under the positive feedback ADR-0079 read, a pair rewarded more often fires more and is selected more, so a reward without information can lock a stimulus onto either of its readouts. Its accuracy is then not the binomial `CONTROL_MAX`'s tail describes, and a clause on it could fail a real learner whenever the coin's lock-in happened to land on the answer for both stimuli. The control for information is the **mirrored pair**: both rewarded arms start from the same image and present the same trials, and only the mapping of the answer differs, so a preference that does not follow the reward's information — a bias of the network, a lock-in on an early choice — cannot put both above 80. The shuffled arm is run and read for what lock-in looks like on this network: per stimulus, the split of its presentations between the two readouts over the last 128 trials and every block, and the four couplings.
- **Why no withheld arm** (option 6). Under the baseline at zero it is the frozen run, and H-13's withheld arm already read the frozen network's selection over 512 trials of the same image and seed (24 to 30 of 64 per block toward the assignment's readout). The calibration below reads the image's first block frozen; the round may add a withheld arm if its cost is small beside the three.
- **Why not a consolidation at the trial's end** (option 7). It is a change to the fan-out's rule, a mechanism; nothing read yet says the reinforced form needs it. It is named below as a candidate if H-14 is a no.

**H-14 (a reward the engine earns moves its selection)**, written into whitepaper §11.1 as a Hypothesis with the following, all fixed here:

- **The network and the calibration.** ADR-0077's settled image, built as ADR-0077 and ADR-0079 build it (`settled_image`), the gain 1.75, the controller off, the modulation baseline zero. Before any rewarded run the round holds the image to ADR-0077's tables and runs its first block frozen, held to ADR-0077's frozen run — the stimulus firing once, the sight 62, the sign 53 — as ADR-0079's calibration did. A mismatch stops the round before any rewarded run and is a finding, not an outcome.
- **The constants.** ADR-0076's stimulus; the instrument's geometry, `WINDOW`, `TRIAL_TICKS`, `SEED`, `REWARD_Q16` (1.0), `BLOCK` (64), `LAST_BLOCKS` (2) and `REWARDED_MIN` (80); and **1 536 trials, twenty-four blocks**.
- **The delivery.** `Task::trial` with `Delivery::Addressed` and the arm's feedback, unchanged: after each trial the synapses from the presented stimulus's units onto the selected readout's units addressed, none at a tie, and the reward $\pm 1.0$ by the feedback.
- **The arms.** Three runs of the same 1 536 trials from the one image: **the assignment** (`Feedback::Answer`, A's answer readout 0 and B's readout 1); **the mirrored assignment** (`Feedback::Answer`, `mirrored`); and **the shuffled reward** (`Feedback::Shuffled`), a reading.
- **The criterion.** Over the last 128 trials, the correct selections (`Outcome::correct`: the selection equal to the stimulus's answer; a tie is not correct) number **at least 80** (`REWARDED_MIN`, by chance about once in 337) **in the assignment and in the mirrored assignment**. H-14 is **yes** when both hold and **no** otherwise.
- **The assertion, not a criterion.** In both rewarded arms every synapse outside the two answer pairs — the two wrong pairs among them — ends the run as the image holds it, bit for bit (derived above); in the shuffled arm, every synapse outside the four stimulus–readout pairs.
- **The readings**, in every arm: the selection per block and per stimulus; the four stimulus–readout couplings per block; the sight per block (whether the readout still sees as the couplings rise); in the shuffled arm, each stimulus's split between its two readouts over the last 128 trials and per block, read as lock-in when a stimulus goes to one readout in the last 128 as often as a rewarded arm goes to its answer; the dopamine signal at the trials' ends; and the trace's composition on the answer pairs by ADR-0079's oracle, at least in the first and the last block. Where the rise stops, if it stops within 1 536 trials, is read and not predicted.
- **The prediction, a Hypothesis written before the run.** H-14 is yes: by the arithmetic above, the selection passes 40 of 64 per block by about trial 900 in both assignments, the mirrored first, and the last 128 read above 80. And the wrong pairs end bit for bit the image's in both rewarded arms. For the shuffled arm no prediction is written.
- **H-14 and H-12, written before the outcome.** H-14's clause is H-12's rewarded clause — 80 of the last 128 in both assignments — asked on a configuration none of H-12's rewarded runs used: the settled network, the baseline at zero and a run of 1 536 trials. H-12 stays closed as the decision its stopping rule reached, and nothing of it is re-decided. If H-14 is yes, the ADR that records it writes, beside H-12's closing sentence in §11.1, that its behavioural question has been answered yes under that configuration, and that ADR-0072's per-trial sign mark (56 of 64), which H-12's rule used as a calibration, was stricter than learning needed on this network, the average sign sufficing (H-13 at 53). If H-14 is no, nothing is written beside H-12.
- **H-14's stopping rule, written with it:**
  1. **One round** (brief 037): the calibration, the three arms, the criterion committed before the first rewarded run.
  2. **A calibration that does not reproduce stops the round** before any rewarded run; it is a finding, not an outcome.
  3. **Yes**: H-14 recorded yes with its scope — this network, this delivery, this regime, this rule, 1 536 trials — and the note beside H-12 as written above. The next decision is an ADR that chooses what the learning line asks next among, at least: the baseline at which every synapse consolidates (does the learning survive plasticity everywhere); the assignment reversed within a run (does a learned selection unlearn, under a feedback that cannot depress); the operating regime (§11.1's open item); and another size. It is not ordered here.
  4. **No**: H-14 recorded no with its scope, and the next decision is an ADR that chooses between a consolidation at the trial's end (ADR-0069's third named change, a rule) and the operating regime, with this round's readings — among them whether the selection was still rising in the last blocks, which is read and is not a licence for a longer run.
  5. **No constant moves after a rewarded run** — not the length, the baseline, the reward, the window, the mark, the feedback or the arms — and there is no second attempt at H-14 in this configuration.

### Consequences

- Good: the reinforced form is asked with the task the engine already has, on the one configuration where the rule's direction has been read, under a criterion, a prediction, a relation to H-12 and a stopping rule written before any run.
- Good: the lock-in question is answered by a derivation the run tests, not by a clause that could fail a learner: in a rewarded arm a wrong pair cannot consolidate, and the shuffled arm shows what lock-in does when the reward carries no information.
- Good: whichever way H-14 reads, the next decision is named.
- Neutral: the run's length is the one constant moved against H-13 besides the delivery, and it is moved by an argument written here from H-13's readings; its figures are estimates, labelled so.
- Bad: three runs of 1 536 trials after a lead-in of ninety-six windows cost about two and a half times ADR-0079's test; the brief lets the round move the runs to a test binary of their own.
- Bad: a yes on H-14 is a claim at 1 024 units on one settled network with plasticity confined to the addressed synapses; everything the line would need to say "the engine learns" beyond that — plasticity everywhere, reversal, size, regime — stays after it.

## Alternatives considered and why rejected

- **512 trials** (option 2): a predicted no that measures the speed the arithmetic already gives.
- **A reward of one sign** (option 3): the leftover signal would reward a wrong selection's pair, the lock-in path, and the task's negative reward already removes it.
- **Brief 027's baseline** (option 4): the attribution the derivation needs would be lost; the question is named for after a yes.
- **The shuffled reward bounded by `CONTROL_MAX`** (option 5): under positive feedback its accuracy is not binomial; the mirrored pair is the information control.
- **A withheld arm** (option 6): H-13's withheld arm read it; the round may add one.
- **A consolidation at the trial's end** (option 7): a mechanism without a measured need; a candidate after a no.
- **Leaving the length to brief 037**: a run's length chosen by the round that runs it, after the lead-in, is a constant whose choice the round could be accused of fitting; it is fixed here from H-13's readings.
