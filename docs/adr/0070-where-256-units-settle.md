# ADR-0070: Where 256 units settle — the excitatory sum read over eighty windows under the drive alone on the instrument's own executor at 256 units; brief 026's clause first holds at the ninth window, where the sum is 0.818 of the prior's, and the sum is still falling at the eightieth, at 0.551 of the prior's by 0.24 per cent per window, so the clause reads a rate and the item is discharged with both numbers; the lead-in the rule derives is nine windows, and behind it the calibration's measure reads 50 of 64 in the first block and 34 to 44 after, so 256 units is not usable for this task behind a lead-in of any length, because the drive alone carries the sum below the level at which the instrument sees and the task adds about one per cent to that; a learning round's criterion stays at 1 024 units; no constant moves, and what would be needed is named and not built

## Context and Problem Statement

[ADR-0055](0055-a-weight-that-settles.md) answered ADR-0053's drain in the pair rule and read the day at both sizes: at 1 024 units under the controller the excitatory sum settles at 0.45 of the prior's over eighty windows, every one of the last sixteen within 0.75 per cent of the sixty-fourth; at 256 units with the gain held over sixteen windows the sum was still falling at the sixteenth, decelerating, and brief 026's clause (each of the last four windows moving the sum by less than two per cent) failed at the 5 000-tick period by 0.27 points. Whitepaper §11.1 kept the item open "for the windows at which 256 units settle". [ADR-0066](0066-the-reward-path-measured-again.md) then met the same fact from the task's side: at 256 units the calibration's measure fell from 57 of 64 in the first block to 31 in the eighth as the excitatory sum fell to 0.55 of the prior's, so the instrument lost the stimulus within a block once the weights moved, and its criterion, brief 030's and [ADR-0069](0069-the-addressed-reward-measured.md)'s, had to stand at 1 024 units alone, where a run costs about four times a 256-unit run.

Brief 031 asked the open item directly and the one question that follows from it: where do 256 units settle, if they settle, and does a lead-in of that many windows under the drive alone, run before the first trial, hold the instrument through a whole run? It changed no rule: not the pair rule, not ADR-0055's depression, not the inhibitory rule, not the controller, not the prior, not one constant of the instrument. The round's content is a measurement, a constant derived from it by a rule written in the brief, and a criterion written before the run. The lead-in is derived, never chosen, and was committed (`b3b3cab`) before the first commit that holds a run behind it (`4a655c7`); the settling measurement it derives from is `f99bd64`.

## Decision Drivers

- [ADR-0010](0010-measured-or-target.md) and principle 3: every number is the engine's own on the reference prior at 256 units in the instrument's configuration, pinned from one run and held on every worker count and architecture; nothing here says anything about 1 024 units, which ADR-0055 and ADR-0066 measured, or about Appendix A's scale.
- The clause is brief 026's, unchanged, applied at every window rather than at the sixteenth alone, as an integer rule over the table; the lead-in is the smallest window that satisfies it or the bound; nothing is chosen after a run (the lesson of ADR-0051, ADR-0054, ADR-0060 and ADR-0066).
- [ADR-0061](0061-the-learning-runs-leave-the-gate.md) and [ADR-0067](0067-the-weekly-dispatch-has-a-scope.md): the heavy runs are weekly `exhaustive` tests; the gate grows by one test that runs no whole run; this round adds tests, so its evidence dispatch is `scope=both`. [ADR-0069](0069-the-addressed-reward-measured.md) warned that the weekly exhaustive job grows with every measuring round; this round measures its time and states it.
- Every loop ends by construction: the settling runs a fixed number of windows and the clause is read afterwards, never "until it settles".
- Latest ≠ Newest (§2.1): no mechanism is added, and in particular not the per-unit synaptic scaling of §8.8, which §11.1 gates on H-8 and whose reason against this problem ADR-0055 already gave.

## Considered Options

1. **The protocol below, written first: the settling measurement over eighty windows, the clause as brief 026 wrote it, the lead-in derived, the rewarded run behind it under a criterion written before the run, its outcome recorded whatever it is.**
2. A lead-in chosen from the settling table by eye (where the per-window move falls under one per cent, or where the sum stops moving to the second decimal) or from the run's own blocks.
3. The settling measurement extended to 160 windows.
4. A mechanism that would hold the sum where the instrument sees: the per-unit gain, the controller at 256 units, a recalibration of the instrument on the settled network.

## Decision Outcome

### Consequences

- Good: §11.1's item from ADR-0055 is discharged with a number either way, and the round's second question is answered with the block at which the measure crosses (the first) and the reason (the measure follows the sum's level, and the drive alone carries the sum below the level the instrument sees).
- Good: later rounds stop spending a 256-unit run as if it could be read; ADR-0069's rule for a size that stops seeing stays the way to read one, and a learning round's criterion stays at 1 024 units, whose cost is now known to be the price of an instrument calibrated on the frozen prior rather than of the size itself.
- Good: the lead-in exists in the harness as a parameter in windows (`run_behind`), derived by a rule with its oracle, so a later round that recalibrates on the settled network has the run it needs.
- Neutral: the gate's runtime tests gain one test of $2^{19}$ ticks at 256 units; the weekly job gains two runs at 256 units.
- Bad: the answer is no, and it is no in a way a lead-in cannot change; the cost of the learning track stays the 1 024-unit run, and the constraint ADR-0069 named on the weekly exhaustive job (it grows with every measuring round and nothing takes a run out of it) is now two runs heavier and still undecided.
- Bad: brief 026's clause, held to be the settling criterion since ADR-0055, holds while the sum still falls by a third; a later round that needs "settled" needs a clause on the level, as ADR-0055's 1 024-unit reading (the last sixteen windows within 0.75 per cent of the sixty-fourth) already is.

## Alternatives considered and why rejected

- **Option 2**, a chosen lead-in: nothing chosen after a run, and the table shows no choice would have met the criterion.
- **Option 3**, 160 windows: permitted only when the clause had not held by the eightieth; it held at the ninth, and the reading says what more windows would have read (a lower level and a slower fall).
- **Option 4**, a mechanism or a recalibration: outside the brief's licence; named above as what a next round would decide, which is the whole of this round's licence about them.
- **Counting the run behind the lead-in against ADR-0066's criterion**: the correct trials are a reading; a reading through a readout that does not see is ADR-0060 again, and ADR-0069's rule already says so.
